How to Calculate Stair Stringers

Calculating stair stringers requires precision to ensure the stairs are safe and code-compliant. First, measure the total vertical rise from the landing to the deck surface. Divide this by your target riser height—typically around 7 inches—to find the number of risers. Once you have the riser count, you must calculate the total horizontal run by multiplying the number of treads by your chosen tread depth. The stringers act as the structural backbone, and their spacing is critical; generally, they should be placed no more than 16 inches on center to support the weight of the treads and live loads effectively.

What Affects Stair Construction

Several factors influence the structural integrity and safety of deck stairs. Local building codes are the primary driver, dictating maximum riser heights and minimum tread depths to prevent tripping hazards. The choice of material also plays a role; pressure-treated lumber, composite decking, and cedar all have different span capacities, which may affect how many stringers you need to install. Additionally, the slope of the terrain and the connection point at the deck ledger board must be carefully managed to ensure the stairs remain stable over time. Improperly cut stringers or inadequate attachment to the deck frame are the most common causes of premature stair failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum allowable riser height for deck stairs?

Most residential building codes, including the IRC, limit the maximum riser height to 7.75 inches to ensure consistent and safe climbing.

How many stringers do I need for a 4-foot wide staircase?

For a 4-foot wide staircase, you typically need at least four stringers, spaced 16 inches apart, to provide adequate support for the tread material.

Can I use composite decking on my stair treads?

Yes, but you must check the manufacturer's span requirements, as composite boards are often more flexible than wood and may require tighter stringer spacing.
