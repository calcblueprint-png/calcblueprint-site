How CalcBlueprint calculates your estimate

Every calculator on this site uses published formulas from industry standards bodies — ACI, NRCA, ICC, ASTM, and manufacturer specifications. No proprietary algorithms, no hidden assumptions. This page explains exactly how each calculation works.

The core principle: published standards, not secret formulas

Construction math isn't proprietary. The formulas for concrete volume, roof pitch, tile coverage, and paint quantity are all defined in publicly available standards. Our job is to apply them correctly and let you adjust the inputs.

That's the opposite of how most construction sites work. They hide their formulas and give you vague ranges. We show you the source standard for every calculator so you can verify it yourself.

Bottom line: our calculators tell you what the published formula says, given your inputs. For engineering decisions or code compliance, always confirm with a licensed contractor or engineer.

How the general formula works

Most material calculators follow a similar structure:

  1. Calculate volume or area from your dimensions (length × width × depth, or length × width)
  2. Apply a material-specific constant (e.g., 27 cubic feet per cubic yard for concrete)
  3. Add a waste factor (typically 5–10% depending on material)
  4. Convert to orderable units (cubic yards, bags, sheets, gallons)
  5. Multiply by your unit price to estimate total cost

Each calculator tailors these steps to its specific material. The waste factor and unit conversion differ by material — for example, tile requires 10% waste for cuts, while concrete typically uses 5–8% for spillage.

Where the numbers come from

  • American Concrete Institute (ACI) — volume and strength standards for concrete
  • National Roofing Contractors Association (NRCA) — roofing material coverage and installation
  • International Code Council (ICC) — IRC and IBC for framing, footings, and structural spans
  • ASTM International — material testing and density standards
  • Manufacturer data — spread rates, coverage specs, and packaging sizes for specific products

How to verify a calculation yourself

You don't have to trust this page. Here's how to sanity-check any of our estimates:

  1. Run a calculation (e.g., Concrete Slab Cost Calculator) with simple inputs — say, 10 ft × 10 ft × 4 in.
  2. Work the math by hand: 10 × 10 × 0.333 ft = 33.3 cubic feet; 33.3 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards.
  3. Compare with our result. With a 5% waste factor, we'll show ~1.30 cubic yards.
  4. Cross-check the standard — ACI 318 publishes the same conversion (1 yd³ = 27 ft³).

What our calculators do NOT do

  • They don't replace an engineer. Structural loads, soil conditions, and code requirements need a licensed professional.
  • They don't check local codes. Building codes vary by jurisdiction. Always verify with your local building department.
  • They don't account for site conditions. Slope, access, and ground stability can all change material requirements.
  • They don't include waste you can't predict. Complex cuts, pattern matching, and damaged material add extra waste we can't foresee.
  • They don't guarantee pricing. Material prices vary by region, supplier, and market conditions.
