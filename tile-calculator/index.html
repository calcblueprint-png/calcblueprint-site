How to Calculate Tile Quantity

The formula is: Room Area ÷ Tile Area = Number of Tiles. Measure room dimensions in feet, convert to inches, and divide. Always add 10% for cuts, breakage, and future repairs.

Coverage by Tile Size

12×12 tiles (12" × 12")

One tile = 1 sq ft. Easy math — a 200 sq ft room needs 200 tiles plus waste.

18×18 tiles

One tile = 2.25 sq ft. Popular for large rooms because grout lines are fewer.

6×6 tiles

One tile = 0.25 sq ft. Used in bathrooms and smaller spaces. More grout lines = more grout needed.

12×24 tiles

One tile = 2 sq ft. Popular for modern kitchens and bathrooms. Requires level substrate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tiles in a box?

Box contents vary by manufacturer. Common box sizes: 12×12 = 10–15 tiles per box; 18×18 = 6–8 tiles per box; 12×24 = 4–6 tiles per box. Always check the box label.

How much thinset do I need?

Plan on one 50 lb bag of thinset for every 30–50 sq ft of floor, depending on trowel size and tile thickness. Large-format tiles need more thinset per square foot.

How much grout do I need?

Grout quantity depends on tile size and joint width. Smaller tiles with wider joints need more grout. A 25 lb bag typically covers 50–200 sq ft depending on tile format.
