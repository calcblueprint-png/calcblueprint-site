How this calculator works

This tool calculates the volume of a continuous footing by multiplying the cross-sectional area (width multiplied by depth) by the total linear length. It then converts this cubic volume into standard ordering units (cubic yards for Imperial, cubic meters for Metric) and automatically applies a recommended 10% waste margin to account for excavation variances and spillage.

How to Calculate Concrete Footing Volume

Calculating the volume of concrete required for a continuous footing is a straightforward process of finding the total cubic volume of the trench. In the imperial system, first convert the footing width and depth from inches to feet by dividing by 12. Multiply these dimensions (Width in feet × Depth in feet × Length in feet) to find the volume in cubic feet, then divide by 27 to convert to cubic yards. For example, a footing measuring 18 inches (45.7 cm) wide, 12 inches (30.5 cm) deep, and 100 feet (30.5 meters) long has a cross-section of 1.5 feet by 1 foot, yielding 150 cubic feet or approximately 5.56 cubic yards (4.25 m³).

In the metric system, convert centimeters to meters by dividing by 100, then multiply the width, depth, and length directly to obtain cubic meters. Always add a 10% waste factor to account for excavation variances, soil absorption, and minor spillage. Ordering exactly the theoretical volume often results in running short on-site, which can lead to cold joints and expensive secondary delivery charges.

What Affects Concrete Footing Requirements

Several critical real-world factors influence the exact volume and specifications of concrete footings. Soil bearing capacity dictates the required width; weaker soils like clay require wider footings (often 24 inches or 61 cm) to distribute the structural load safely, whereas stable soils may only require 12 to 16 inches (30 to 41 cm). Local climate determines the frost line depth. To prevent frost heave, footings must be placed below this line, which can range from a shallow 12 inches (30 cm) in southern regions to over 48 inches (122 cm) in northern climates.

Additionally, trench excavation is rarely perfectly uniform. Rough, hand-dug, or machine-excavated trenches often have irregular walls and bottoms, which can easily increase the actual concrete volume needed by 5% to 15% over the theoretical mathematical calculation. Proper formwork and soil compaction are essential to prevent the trench walls from bowing under the immense weight of wet concrete, which is approximately 150 lbs/cu ft (2,400 kg/m³).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the standard size for a residential concrete footing?
Standard residential footings for single-family homes are typically 16 to 20 inches (41 to 51 cm) wide and 8 to 12 inches (20 to 30 cm) deep. However, local building codes and specific soil conditions always dictate the final dimensions.
How much extra concrete should I order for waste?
It is highly recommended to add a 10% waste factor to your net calculation. For very irregular trenches or rough excavations, increasing this margin to 15% is safer to prevent short-pouring, which can cause structural joints and costly delivery delays.
How heavy is the concrete poured into a footing?
Standard wet concrete weighs approximately 150 pounds per cubic foot (2,400 kg/m³). A single cubic yard of concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds (1,837 kg), which requires robust formwork and proper safety planning during the pour.
