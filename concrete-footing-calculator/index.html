How to Calculate Concrete Footings

Calculating the volume of concrete needed for structural footings is a straightforward process based on volume geometry. To find the cubic footage of your footing, you must first convert all measurements into feet. Divide your footing width in inches by 12, and divide your footing depth in inches by 12. Once you have these decimal values, multiply them by the total continuous length of the footing in feet.

The formula is expressed as: Volume (Cubic Feet) = (Width / 12) × (Depth / 12) × Length. Because commercial concrete is ordered and delivered in cubic yards, you must convert this figure by dividing the total cubic feet by 27 (since there are 27 cubic feet in a cubic yard). Finally, always add a 10% waste factor to account for uneven excavation trenches, spillage, and settling.

What Affects Concrete Footing Requirements

Several real-world factors can significantly alter the actual amount of concrete your project demands compared to theoretical calculations. Soil conditions play a massive role; rough, hand-dug, or machine-excavated trenches rarely have perfectly smooth, straight walls. Loose soil can cave in or compress under the weight of wet concrete, increasing the volume required to fill the space.

Additionally, local building codes dictate the depth of footings based on the regional frost line. Footings must be placed below this line to prevent frost heaving, which can require deeper excavations and more concrete. Structural loads from the building itself determine the required width and thickness. Lastly, formwork deflection—where wooden forms bow outward under the intense hydrostatic pressure of wet concrete—can easily consume an extra 5% to 10% of material beyond your initial estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How deep do concrete footings need to be?
Footing depth is primarily determined by your local climate's frost line and local building codes. In cold climates, footings must extend below the frost depth (often 36 to 48 inches) to prevent shifting. For standard residential structures, a minimum thickness of 12 inches is common, but always consult your local building department.
2. Why should I add a 10% waste factor to my order?
Adding 10% is standard industry practice. Excavations are never perfectly uniform, and concrete can easily escape through gaps in formwork or sink into soft subgrade soils. Ordering exactly the theoretical amount almost always results in running short, which requires an expensive second delivery and creates weak cold joints in your foundation.
3. When should I use pre-mixed bags versus ordering a ready-mix truck?
For small projects requiring less than 1 cubic yard (approx. 45 bags of 80lb concrete), mixing bags on-site with a portable mixer is highly cost-effective. For any project exceeding 1 cubic yard, ordering ready-mix concrete from a local batch plant saves immense physical labor and ensures structural consistency throughout the pour.
