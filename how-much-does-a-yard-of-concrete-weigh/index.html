How Much Does a Yard of Concrete Weigh?

By Emiliano · Last updated 2026-11-13

A cubic yard of concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds, or 2.02 short tons. That's the number ready-mix suppliers use for delivery weight and what you need for structural load calculations, truck limits, and trailer ratings. But that number shifts based on whether the concrete is wet or cured, its PSI rating, and whether it's normal, lightweight, or heavyweight mix.

Quick math: 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet. Standard concrete density = 150 lb/ft³ wet or 145 lb/ft³ cured. So 27 × 150 = 4,050 lb (wet) or 27 × 145 = 3,915 lb (cured).

Why the Weight Matters

Concrete weight is not trivia — it drives three real-world decisions. Ready-mix delivery is priced and scheduled by the cubic yard, but the truck is limited to about 10 cubic yards (roughly 20 tons) per load before it exceeds federal and state weight limits on residential roads. That's why large pours arrive in multiple trucks, and why pouring more than 10 yards in a single day usually requires a pumper or a two-load schedule.

Structural design uses concrete weight as dead load. A 4-inch slab on grade weighs about 50 pounds per square foot, which a typical residential foundation handles easily. But a 6-inch structural slab on a second-floor deck weighs 75 pounds per square foot, and that becomes part of the total load calculation that determines beam sizing and column spacing.

DIY safety is the third. If you're picking up bagged concrete in a pickup truck, the payload limit for a standard half-ton (F-150, Silverado 1500, Ram 1500) is about 1,500–2,000 lbs. That's 37–50 bags of 80 lb concrete, or about 1.2–1.6 cubic yards. Loading a full yard of concrete (4,050 lbs) into a half-ton pickup will exceed the truck's payload and flatten the rear suspension. Rent a trailer or schedule delivery.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a cubic yard of concrete weigh?

A cubic yard of standard ready-mix concrete weighs about 4,050 pounds, or 2.02 short tons. That is based on 150 pounds per cubic foot for wet concrete. Once cured, the same yard weighs approximately 3,900 to 4,050 pounds depending on the mix and moisture content.

How much does a cubic foot of concrete weigh?

One cubic foot of wet concrete weighs 150 pounds. Cured concrete weighs 145 pounds per cubic foot for standard 4,000 PSI mix. Lightweight concrete can weigh 110 to 120 pounds per cubic foot; heavyweight concrete can exceed 200 pounds per cubic foot.

Does the PSI strength change the weight?

Yes, but only slightly. Higher PSI mixes contain more cement and less water, so they are marginally heavier. A 2,500 PSI mix weighs about 3,950 pounds per cubic yard; a 5,000 PSI mix weighs about 4,150 pounds per cubic yard. The difference is typically 3 to 5 percent across the full range.

Does wet concrete weigh more than cured concrete?

Yes. Wet concrete weighs about 150 pounds per cubic foot because it still contains unreacted water. As concrete cures over 28 days, some water evaporates and some is chemically bound. Cured concrete weighs approximately 145 pounds per cubic foot, or 3.3 percent less than wet.
